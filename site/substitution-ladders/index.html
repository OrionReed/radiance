substitution ladders × Christoffel laws

A cascade cuts every ray into shorter ones: a tile of length P is cut at planes s₁ < … < sk−1, and each piece is cut again, down to the pixel. That rule is the ladder. Plain HRC halves every tile.

A substitution ladder reads the cuts off a rule on letters. Under a → ab, b → a, a tile of letter a is cut into an a and a b, and a b is an a one level down. The lengths are the Fibonacci numbers, and N, the length of the top word, is the size of the grid. Binary is a → aa.

A Christoffel law says where a ray of rise D crosses each cut. It follows the ray's own line, of slope D/P, through one random point U of its start row, so at plane s it is in row ⌊s·D/P + U⌋. That is the Christoffel word of D/P, read at the cuts. Its mean is s·D/P exactly, wherever s falls.

So the two fit together at any cut. Every substitution ladder with the Christoffel law is unbiased, reciprocal and right on solid lights. On a → aa it is plain HRC, to the bit.

Box windows instead join a random point of the start row to a random point of the end row. Their mean is right only at the midpoint, s = P/2. At any other cut it is off by an amount that depends on D, and that error shows as wedges. Switch the law on Fibonacci, and then on binary, where nothing changes.

Measured for Fibonacci at N = 89 and 144 (no wedges, solid lights right, spokes as the balanced cut's, more leak and penumbra); argued for the other rules. Tests are drawn for 128²; at other N they are redrawn from their scene units.
