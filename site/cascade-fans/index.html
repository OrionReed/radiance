fans of lattice cones

Plain's tree never uses the metric, so it runs unchanged in any lattice cone spanned by pixel vectors u, v with det(u, v) = 1: a ray of level L goes from a probe to one of the L + 1 points L u … L v. A fan is a set of such cones covering every direction once; plain's four frustums are one. Every fan is exactly reciprocal.

Count. Let x′ be a cone's level, linear and 1 at u and v, and W(r) = |rx| + |ry| the pixel's width across r, times |r|. Probed at pixel centres, the rays of direction r cover a pixel x′(r) / W(r) times. So solid lights are counted exactly iff x′ = W on every cone: the fan is W's normal fan, the quadrants, generated by ±e₁, ±e₂. In the inset each segment u v must lie on the diamond W = 1. Inside it, lights read bright (red under Δ exact); outside, dark (blue).

Cost. The count fixes the fan's shape; the cost law prices it. Rays per pixel per octave = ∫ dθ / (sp sθ) ≥ 2π / smax², s the spacing of rays, and a lattice cone pays 1. The quadrant fan has half plain's cones and √2 its widest spacing, so the same merit, 1.27: it is plain made coarser, not a cheaper plain.
