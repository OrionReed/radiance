reciprocal HRC

There is no ½ near/far blend on even planes and no reprojection, and each ray stores one merged radiance. The merge is reciprocal: light from p to q equals light from q to p, with any walls (K=KT before the solid mask and the row blur).

As in HRC's extensions, a ray is two half-length rays chained at its midpoint. When the halves can't have the same slope, the ray averages both bent orders, as HRC builds its odd rays.

The merge runs the same pairs of halves backwards, from the top cascade down, with the same weights. A ray on an even plane (a left half) takes its parent's merged radiance M as it is: the near-plane read. A ray on an odd plane (a right half) takes it through the left half, plus the left half's own radiance times the parent's cone weight c.
