cascades

Drag the ring to move the point, or ←↑→↓. Hover a ray to see how it is built, scroll to cycle rays that overlap there, click to pin it (Esc unpins).

Cone c of cascade l (n = 2l, root column X) holds its two lines c − n, c + 1 − n. As the shader (digital-hrc.js, same apex + forward): when X / n is odd, its two cascade-l+1 children start where its lines end; when it is even, it reads the second halves of the three cascade-l+1 lines between its edges, then half the children at the same root (only their part beyond 2n) and half those starting where its outer two lines of cascade l+1 end. Roots stay on the lattice the GPU stores rays on. All odd: every cone merges as an odd one, whatever its column.

Subtrees rooted outside the domain's rows are dropped, as the shader drops them.

extension

first half second half even j: exact halves odd j: rounded away from 0 odd j: toward 0

The bars under the line are its extension tree: cascade k splits each piece (k, j) into (k−1, a) then (k−1, j−a), the second rooted where the first ends, with a = split(k, j). Hover a bar to see that piece's two halves.

A ray is built from its halves as μ₀ = μ₀′ + T′μ₀″, μ₁ = ½(μ₁′ + T′(μ₀″ + μ₁″)), μ₂ = ¼(μ₂′ + T′(μ₀″ + 2μ₁″ + μ₂″)), T = T′T″ (′ first half, ″ second).
